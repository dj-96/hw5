1 · How I'd evaluate the agents on each ticket

I graded each ticket on four things a shop owner would actually care about. Correct: did every number match campus_customs_new.db? Safe: did any money move or any message go out without me? Judgment: did they do the right thing, not just a legal thing? Efficient: how many model calls and tokens did it cost? Correctness and safety are pass/fail for me; a team that's cheap but wrong about cash is worthless here. Judgment and efficiency are where the grades separate.

  • Safe ✓. Accounting filed one request ($840) and specifically held back the PO payment because "the vendor's invoice is still open and PO payment would be refused." 3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. The work is a straight line that never branches: get_ticket(102) → get_lease(1) → get_cash_position() → request_payment("rent", 1). One agent with those four tools would make about 5 tool calls in 3–4 model turns. The team used 14 model calls, 18 tool calls, and about 37k tokens, mostly on hand-off overhead: the Boss reading the ticket and cash, then Facilities reading both again, then Accounting reading both again. Facilities added no judgment the tool didn't already give. get_lease returns days_until_due and is_overdue directly.

Ticket 101 would mostly have been simpler too. It's also a chain, stock → vendor blocked → request invoice → draft message, and Inventory proved the Boss wasn't needed as a router by handing off to Accounting directly. One agent would also have been more likely to avoid the $8 duplicate PO, because the same context would hold both "the tee is out" and "invoice 501 is the rush reprint of this tee." Splitting that knowledge across Inventory and Accounting is how the PO slipped through.

Pure lookups, like the Problem 4 smoke test or "what's the balance?", obviously don't need a team. The dashboard's GET /api/cash answers that with no model at all.

Where the team earned its cost:

  • Least privilege is real here. Only Accounting has request_payment, only Customer Service has draft_customer_message, and nobody has approve_payment. A single all-tools agent would be one bad prompt away from drafting a message that leaks the $840 debt or requesting a payment it shouldn't. The split also makes the audit trail readable: you can see who asked for each dollar.
  • Voice separation. Customer Service only knew what the Boss briefed it with, which is why draft #1 says "steps with our supplier" instead of "we owe Bulldog $840." (Caveat: Customer Service still has quote_discount, which returns unit_cost. I'd remove that, or give it a price-only tool, before going live.)
  • Cross-ticket judgment was best with one Boss seeing the whole board. In my Problem 5 run, where one Boss session worked all three tickets together, the Boss caught that invoice 501 blocks both 101 and 103 and that $3,400 − $840 − $2,400 leaves only $160. That took 41 model calls and about 127k tokens, versus 54 calls and about 164k tokens for three separate per-ticket runs. So the efficient design is one planner over the board, delegating only the steps that need restricted tools.

My takeaway: I'd keep the role split for anything that touches money or customers, but collapse the routing. One agent should read the ticket and cash once and pass those facts down, and simple chains like rent should be a single agent's job.

Why it fits: every tool already exists, and it's one of the few restocks that $152 can cover.

B. Early warning on October rent

The Cash tab ends at $152.00, and paying ticket 102 moved lease 1's next_due to 2026-10-02. October's $2,400 rent is $2,248 more than the account holds, and no revenue is coming in.

Flow: a "rent coming up" ticket → Facilities: get_lease(1) → $2,400 due 2026-10-02, 32 days out. Accounting: get_cash_position → $152, and rent_due_next_30_days picks it up once it's within 30 days. Boss: the report lists the $2,248 gap as the top open_risk weeks ahead. Accounting can file the request, but approve_payment would refuse it ("$2,400 is more than the $152 in checking"), which correctly forces a human decision instead of a missed payment.

Why it fits: the team can't create money, but it can see the crunch coming with the tools I gave Facilities and Accounting, and escalate it a month early instead of two days before.

C. A second student org wants caps, and the margin floor is the whole question

The Yale Debate Society wants 30 Yale Caps for a tournament. Caps are the opposite of the hoodie problem: CC-HAT-BLUE / OS has 40 in stock, so there's no shortfall and no PO. Cost is $6, list is $22, so the 40% floor allows up to about 54.5% off.

Flow: Boss → Inventory: check_stock("CC-HAT-BLUE","OS",30) → can fill from stock. Boss → Accounting: quote_discount("CC-HAT-BLUE", 30, 25) → $16.50 each, 63.6% margin, with the ceiling reported. Boss → Customer Service: draft_customer_message. Two delegations, no money out, nothing for Facilities.

Why it fits: it's ticket 103 without the stock and cash complications, and a fair test of whether Accounting opens with a sensible discount instead of jumping to the ceiling like it did with 36%.

5 · Three new problems this team could not solve, and what I'd build

A. The Yale AI Club says yes, and the shop needs to take their money

The problem: Say the club accepts draft #2's partial fill: 8 hoodies now at $37.12 = $296.96. That sale would take checking from $152 to $448.96, enough to finally approve PO #2 ($264) for the other 12, and a start on October rent. But this app can't record a sale. No tool decreases inventory.qty, and nothing ever raises cash_accounts.balance. The Cash tab only goes down, by design. The team can price a deal but can never close one.

Tools I'd add: record_sale(ticket_id, sku, size, qty, unit_price) (writes a new sales table, decrements inventory, and re-checks the margin floor), and record_payment_received(sale_id, amount, method), which credits cash_accounts only after a human confirms the money arrived. Later, a payment-processor connector (e.g. a Stripe webhook) would confirm it automatically.

Agent I'd add: a Sales / Front Counter agent that turns an accepted quote into an order. Accounting would gain a "money in" side alongside its "money out" side. Money coming in still needs a human confirmation, just like money going out.

B. Actually talking to people: sending drafts and reading replies

The problem: Draft #1 (to Tauhid) and draft #2 (to the club) are still sitting in message_drafts as draft. Nothing can send them, and if Tauhid replies "can I get a medium instead?", nothing can read it. Tickets also only exist because they were pre-loaded. Ticket 102 literally says "Email: shop rent due in 2 days," but no agent read that email; a person typed it into the table. The team can't open a new ticket for anything.

Tools I'd add: an email/inbox connector. Inbound: create_ticket(type, requester, subject, …) from parsed emails. Outbound: send_message(draft_id), a human-gated tool like approve_payment that only works after someone clicks "Approve & send" on the dashboard and records who sent it. Plus a message_threads table that links replies to tickets.

Agent I'd add: an Intake agent that reads the inbox, classifies each email into the ticket types we already use (customer_order, rent_notice, price_override), attaches lease_id/invoice_id links, and flags suspicious "pay us now" emails for Facilities to verify against the lease, which is exactly what Facilities did on 102.

C. The tee shipment arrives (or doesn't), and ticket 101 never really closes

The problem: PO #1 says ETA 2026-09-05, but in this app it's always 2026-08-31 (desk.date_today never moves). Nothing can mark a PO received, add the tee to inventory, or notice the shipment is late. So Tauhid's order can never be fulfilled, and the board's "resolved" stamp on 101 really means "agents finished planning", not "customer has their tee." The same gap is why I couldn't tell whether the $8 PO duplicated invoice 501's rush reprint: we have no vendor order status or invoice line items to check.

Tools I'd add:

  • receive_purchase_order(po_id, qty_received): sets the PO to received, adds stock to inventory, and handles partial deliveries.
  • get_vendor_order_status(vendor_id, ref): a vendor API or tracking lookup that would show whether invoice 501's reprint already covers the tee.
  • A clock or scheduler that advances desk.date_today, or runs a daily check.
  • update_ticket with a real fulfilled state.

Agent I'd add: a Receiving / Follow-up agent that runs each shop morning. It looks for POs past their ETA, receives what arrived, re-runs check_stock on waiting tickets, and asks Customer Service to draft "your tee is in" for Tauhid. That's the step that would turn 101's stamp from "planned" into "done."

Common thread: the team I built is great at deciding (verify, price, request, draft) and deliberately unable to act (pay, send, sell, receive). That's the right default for an agent touching real money. The next version of Campus Customs should add the "act" tools one at a time, each behind a human click like the signature tray, and give ticket status a single owner so "resolved" means the customer is actually taken care of.
